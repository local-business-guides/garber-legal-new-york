Choosing a personal injury attorney in New York City

Before you hire a lawyer, gather the basic facts and ask how the firm will handle communication, records, and the next steps in your case.

By Garber LegalUpdated 2026-10-27New York, New York

## What to look for in a personal injury attorney
Start with your own situation. Garber Legal represents people dealing with injuries connected to automobile accidents, falls, construction incidents, and medical malpractice. When you speak with a personal injury attorney, explain what happened in your own words and ask how the firm approaches cases like yours. You should come away with a clear sense of who will be your point of contact and how you can reach them.

You can also ask what information the firm needs before it can assess your situation. A first conversation is a chance to understand the process, not a reason to feel pressured to have every answer ready. If something is unclear, ask for it to be explained in plain language.

## Questions to ask before hiring
Use your conversation to learn how the working relationship will go. Consider asking:

  • Who will answer questions as the matter moves forward?
  • How will I receive updates, and how often should I expect to hear from the firm?
  • What documents or details would help you review my situation?
  • What decisions might I need to make, and when?
  • How do you prepare if a matter needs to be presented in court?

A trial attorney can explain how preparation for court fits into the work on a case. Ask about that process without assuming that every claim follows the same path. Good communication matters from the first call through any later decisions.

## A simple checklist for your first conversation
Before you call, write down a short timeline. Include when and where the incident happened, what you remember, and when you first sought care. If you don't know an exact date or detail, say so rather than guessing.

Gather what you already have, but don't delay a conversation because your folder isn't complete. Useful items may include:

  • Photos of the scene, property, or injuries
  • Medical paperwork and appointment details
  • Names and contact information for witnesses
  • Messages or letters from an insurer or another involved party
  • Notes about missed work or changes to everyday activities

Keep original documents where they are, and make copies or take clear photos for your own records. Don't edit images or add assumptions to your timeline. A personal injury attorney can tell you what else may be useful after hearing the facts.

## Mistakes to avoid, and when to call
Try not to rely on memory alone as days pass. Write down new details while they're fresh, and keep records of conversations about the incident. Avoid posting guesses or private details about the matter online. If an insurer or another party contacts you, you can ask for time to understand what is being requested before responding.

Contact a professional when an injury has disrupted your routine, the cause is disputed, or you aren't sure what to do with calls and paperwork. Questions people ask about personal injury attorney

What should I bring when I contact a personal injury attorney?

A brief timeline, medical information, photographs, and any letters or messages you have can help start the discussion. If you don't have those items yet, you can still call and ask what to gather.

Should I wait until I know all the facts before calling?

You don't need a complete account to ask for guidance. Share what you know, identify what remains uncertain, and ask what details could be helpful to collect.

What should I ask about communication with the firm?

Ask who will be your regular contact and how updates are usually provided. It is also reasonable to ask how to send questions or documents as they come up.
