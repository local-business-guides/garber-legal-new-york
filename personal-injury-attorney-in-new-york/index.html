Hiring a personal injury attorney in New York

An injury can leave you sorting out paperwork, care, and questions about what happened. A focused first conversation can help you organize the facts and consider what to do next.

Start with the details of the incident

When you contact a personal injury attorney in New York, describe the event in a straightforward way. Note the location, approximate time, people involved, and what you observed before and after the injury. In a city setting, it may help to identify a street, intersection, building entrance, work site, or other clear landmark. You don't need to prove your account during the first call. The goal is to share enough context for a useful discussion.

Garber Legal handles matters involving automobile accidents, slip and falls, construction mishaps, and medical malpractice. Explain what kind of incident occurred, and mention any parts of the story that are uncertain or disputed. Clear details help the firm understand what questions may need follow-up.

Keep a record of calls and paperwork

After an incident, different people or organizations may contact you. Keep a simple log with the date, the person or organization, what was discussed, and any requested follow-up. Save letters, emails, claim information, and notes in one place. If you speak by phone, write down the main points soon afterward.

Before signing or sending something you don't understand, take time to read it and ask what it means. Don't guess at answers or fill gaps with details you can't confirm. If a form asks for information you don't have, note that and ask how to handle it. A personal injury attorney in New York can review the information you have and discuss questions about the situation.

Prepare for the first meeting

A little preparation can make the conversation more useful. You might bring or have ready:

  • A short sequence of events, including dates you can confirm
  • Photos, videos, or the names of people who saw what happened
  • Details about medical visits and instructions you received
  • Information about work or daily tasks affected by the injury
  • Any messages or documents from insurers, property owners, employers, or others

Keep your notes factual. Separate what you personally saw from what someone else told you, and mark estimates as estimates. If the incident happened at a location you can identify, jot down the address or nearby landmark. Don't put off asking for help just because you still need to collect some records.

Know when to ask for legal guidance

Consider speaking with a lawyer if you have questions about who may be responsible, if the account of the incident is being challenged, or if handling the paperwork feels difficult while you are managing an injury. It can also help to seek guidance when you aren't sure how to respond to contact from an insurer or another party. Timing and rules can depend on the circumstances, so ask about your situation rather than relying on a general assumption.
